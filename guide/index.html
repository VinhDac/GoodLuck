The GoodLuck! guide

Everything you need to set GoodLuck! up and use it every day. Setting up takes about ten minutes. After that, the app does the daily work of a job search and you spend your time on the jobs worth it.

Try it as you readMost of this guide can be tried in the demo: the real app, running in your browser on fictional data.

1. Install

On a Mac

  1. Download GoodLuck-1.3.0.dmg from the download page and open it.
  2. Drag GoodLuck onto Applications.
  3. Open it from Applications or Launchpad.

The first time, macOS may say it cannot check the app, because it does not come from the App Store. Open System Settings, then Privacy & Security, scroll down and press Open Anyway. You only do this once.

GoodLuck! needs macOS 12 or later, and Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge or Brave, which it uses in the background to read job sites and to print PDFs. It comes with everything else it needs on a Mac with Apple silicon; on a Mac with an Intel processor, install Python 3.11 or later from python.org first.

On Windows

  1. Download GoodLuck-Setup-1.3.0.exe from the download page.
  2. Run it and follow the steps. It installs GoodLuck! for you alone, with no administrator rights needed, and adds it to the Start menu; tick Create a desktop shortcut if you want one there too.
  3. Open GoodLuck from the Start menu.

If Windows says it protected your PC, press More info, then Run anyway.

GoodLuck! needs Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit). It reads job sites with Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge, which comes with Windows.

2. Setting up

On its first launch GoodLuck! opens Setting up: your profile in three parts, then two connections. The app opens once everything required is done.

  1. Who you are. Your name, contact email, phone, where you are based, nationality and education. Where you are based decides which jobs count as near you, and your education counts towards your scores.
  2. What you want. The job titles you would take, your skills, the levels you would accept, where you want to work, where you can work without a visa sponsor, and your working arrangement. This is what the app searches for.
  3. Work experience. How many years you have really worked; then, if you like, your jobs and your projects.
  4. The job mailbox (required). The mailbox you give employers, which the app reads to keep your applications up to date. It has to be the address you gave as your contact email. See Track.
  5. Your phone (optional). A Telegram bot that tells you about new jobs and replies. See Your phone.

The quickest start is Import your CV (.docx or PDF) at the top of Setting up: GoodLuck! reads your CV and offers what it found for the empty answers. Each page keeps its answers when you press its Save button.

Good to knowThe first time, GoodLuck! reads real postings to build its lists of job titles and skills. It takes about half a minute, and the lists appear when it is done; carry on with the other questions meanwhile.

When Setting up is done, the app opens on Home. Press Start session to begin; see Sessions.

3. Your profile

The Profile tab holds everything the app knows about what you want. Two answers matter more than any other: your job titles and your skills. See it in the demo

Job titles: the more wordings, the more jobs

Your job titles decide which postings are kept. A posting whose title does not contain one of yours is set aside, so the wordings employers use matter: Quant Researcher and Quantitative Researcher are two different titles to a job board.

  • You do not have to think of them all. The suggestions under the box are real titles from real postings, and they follow what you pick: choose Quantitative Researcher and its family comes up, from Quant Researcher to Quantitative Research Analyst. Keep clicking and the list keeps following.
  • Type a few letters of each word, in any order: quant res finds Quantitative Researcher.
  • Nothing fits? Press Enter and the title is added exactly as you typed it.
  • Start broad. Ten to twenty titles is a good start. You can narrow them later; a title you never added is a job you never see.

Skills: what your CV does not already say

Scores are worked out from your CV first. Your skills add what the CV does not mention. The suggestions under the box are skills that real postings ask for, and they follow your picks too: choose pandas and the skills postings ask for alongside it come up, such as numpy and scikit learn. Add only what you could talk about in an interview.

Levels, places and working arrangement

  • Levels you'd accept, from Intern / Placement to Lead / Staff / Principal. If you tick only junior levels, a posting whose title says it is senior is set aside.
  • Where do you want to work? Countries, one by one. Add Anywhere (remote) to keep remote work from any country too.
  • Where can you work without a visa sponsor? In any other country, a posting that says it does not sponsor visas is set aside. Need a sponsor everywhere? Choose Nowhere (I need a sponsor).
  • Working arrangement: Fully remote, Hybrid, On-site. Tick On-site and nothing is set aside for its arrangement; tick only Fully remote and a posting not marked remote is set aside.

Nothing set aside is deleted. On the Search tab, Dropped lists those postings with the reason for each, and Keep it brings one back.

4. Your CV

On the Profile tab, press Import a CV (.docx or PDF) and choose the file. GoodLuck! reads it straight away and shows what it found under Approve what goes in: your contact details, your education, the skills it recognised, the titles of the jobs you have held, and more. Only empty answers are offered. Tick what you want and press Save selected.

A PDF has to carry its text: a scan is pictures of pages, with nothing in it to read. Your CV stays on your computer, and GoodLuck! does not change it.

The CV tab

The CV tab measures your CV against the jobs you want. It lists what those jobs keep asking for that your CV never says, with the number of jobs that would reach your mark if it did. See it in the demo

  • Each row quotes the employers who ask for it, so you can see the context.
  • A word that is not a skill can be struck off with the cross beside it: it then stops counting in every score. Not counted lists what you struck off, and ↺ puts one back.
  • Save report writes the findings into your Downloads folder: a PDF to read, or a Markdown file (.md) to give an AI assistant together with your CV.

5. Sessions: how GoodLuck! works for you

Press Start session on the bar at the top of Home. A session runs a round straight away, then one every hour:

  1. Search. It searches job sites for your job titles in the countries you chose, reads companies' job boards and the job alerts in your mailbox, keeps the postings that match your titles, levels and places, and scores every new one.
  2. Manage mail. It reads the mail about your applications and moves each one along.

While a round runs, the bar at the top lights up and the journal shows what is happening. While a session is on, the bar says LIVE, and the status line at the bottom says when the next round is due.

  • Run a round now runs one immediately. Stop ends the session.
  • The key. While a session is on, the GoodLuck! key sits in the menu bar on a Mac, and by the clock on Windows (it may be under the ^ arrow). Its menu says what the app is doing and offers Open GoodLuck!, Run a round now, End session and Quit GoodLuck!. On Windows, a click on the key brings the window back.
  • Closing the window while a session is on hides it, and the session carries on. With no session, closing the window quits the app.
  • When the app opens (Settings, General) chooses whether it waits for you or starts a session by itself.
Leave it onA session is what makes GoodLuck! worth having. Start one in the morning and let it run; it asks nothing of you until there is something to look at.

Job sites

GoodLuck! reads job sites in a browser window of its own, without signing in, during the hours set in Settings, Run (8:00 to 22:00 unless you change them) and at the pace set there. A posting from a job site or a job alert gets its score once its page has been read.

The ⚟ button on Search or on Home turns each source on or off.

6. Reading a score

Every kept job gets a score from 0 to 100: the share of what the posting asks for that your profile and CV show. A posting asking for 27 things, 20 of which your CV and skills mention, scores 74.

Open a job and Why this score lists every ask, how often the posting says it, and the line of your CV that proves it, or nothing on your profile says it. That list is the fastest way to prepare an application. Open a best score in the demo

  • Your mark is the score a job has to reach to be worth your time: 70 unless you change it in Settings, General. Jobs at or above it are your best scores.
  • No score? When a posting asks for fewer than four things the app recognises, there is too little to measure, and the job shows without a number: read it yourself. A posting from a job site or a job alert has no score until its page has been read.
  • A score is not a verdict. It says how much of the posting your CV already covers, so you can decide quickly. The decision stays yours.

7. Search

The Search tab lists the jobs the app has found. It opens on the kept ones, best score first, with the jobs you applied to and the expired ones out of the way. See it in the demo

  • Kept, Dropped, All: what matched your profile, what was set aside, or everything.
  • The search box finds a title or a company.
  • The score slider narrows the list to a range; drag its left end to your mark to see only best scores.
  • Place: anywhere, near you, one of your countries, or remote.
  • Two rows of chips narrow it by who posted the job (a direct employer or an agency) and by where it came from (a company board, a job site or a job alert).
  • Sort by score, newest, oldest, company or title.
  • Applied and Expired show the jobs kept out of the list. Not read yet shows only the jobs without a score yet.

A job's page

Each job opens on its own page: the score and why, then Open the original posting, with a link to the posting on the employer's site. Apply there, then press I applied, and the job moves to Track. If the posting has closed, press Expired. The app also marks a posting expired by itself once it finds it closed.

8. Track

The Track tab is every application you have sent, and where each one stands. See it in the demo

Connect your mailbox

Track reads the mailbox you give employers. It connects once, during Setting up, with an app password, never your account password. With Gmail:

  1. Turn on 2-Step Verification on your Google account if it is not on yet.
  2. Open myaccount.google.com/apppasswords and create an app password. Google shows 16 letters.
  3. In GoodLuck!, on The job mailbox, press Connect, paste the 16 letters and press Connect.

The mailbox has to be the address you gave as your contact email on Profile. Later, Settings, Gmail shows the connected mailbox and lets you change its app password. The password is kept in your system's own vault: Keychain on a Mac, Credential Manager on Windows. The app only reads mail; it never sends any.

How applications move

  • Mail is read at every round. To read it more often, turn on Watch the mailbox in Settings, Gmail.
  • An acknowledgement from an employer adds the application, or marks it applied. An invitation moves it on to interview, a rejection marks it rejected, an offer marks it offer.
  • Applications are grouped by how they stand: They are talking to you, Still inside the reply window, Treated as rejected and Settled.
  • An application that stays silent past the time you choose (Treat as rejected after, at the ⚟ button on Track, 20 days unless you change it) is treated as rejected, so the list says honestly what is still alive.
  • Mail the app cannot place on its own waits in the Queue, where you decide in one click.
  • For a job the app found, I applied on its page adds the application at once. Any other application is added when the employer's mail about it arrives.

9. Home

Home answers the questions of the day at a glance. See it in the demo

  • Best score: the jobs worth applying to, newest found first.
  • Last 7 days: new jobs (the best scores among them bright) and applications sent, day by day. The expand button in its corner opens the last 30 days, then a table of every day something happened, with a total for each column.
  • Applications: where all of them stand, what was sent today, and the replies of the last 14 days.
  • The journal along the bottom of Home, Search and Track: what the app is doing, line by line.

10. Your phone

Connect a Telegram bot and GoodLuck! writes to you: the new jobs each search kept (best score first), the applications that moved, a stage that could not run, and a report at the end of the day. At night, outside the hours set in Settings, Run, the messages arrive without a sound.

Connect the bot

  1. In Telegram, open @BotFather and send /newbot. Choose a name, then a username ending in bot. BotFather answers with a token: copy it.
  2. In GoodLuck!, open Settings, then Notifications, paste the token and press Save. The app gives you a link to your bot.
  3. Open the link, press Start, then press Save again.
  4. Press Test: a message arrives on your phone.

What you can ask it

CommandWhat it does
/jobsFind jobs, with Search's filters: words, 70+, 7d, remote, a country.
/jobOne job, by its number.
/applicationsYour applications and where they stand.
/statusToday's numbers and the session.
/runRun a round now.
/stopStop the session.
/mailMail waiting for your decision.
/cv, /reportSend you your CV, or the CV report. Both ask before sending.

Only your own chat is answered, and no command ever applies for you.

11. Ten minutes a day

GoodLuck! is built for steady progress rather than one long afternoon. A routine that works:

  1. In the morning, open Home. The newest best scores are at the top.
  2. Pick two or three. Open each, read Why this score, apply on the employer's site, press I applied.
  3. Glance at the Queue if it shows a number, and decide on the mail waiting there.
  4. Once a week, expand Last 7 days, and look at the CV tab: one line added to your CV can bring a dozen jobs over your mark.

Leave the session on and let it watch. It does not get tired, and it does not forget to check.

13. When something is wrong

The app will not open

On a Mac, open System Settings, Privacy & Security, and press Open Anyway; on a Mac with an Intel processor, install Python 3.11 or later first. On Windows, press More info, then Run anyway. See Install.

It says GoodLuck! is already open, or already running

Only one copy runs at a time. On Windows, opening it again brings back the window. On a Mac with a session on, use the key in the menu bar, then Open GoodLuck!. If it says another copy is already running, quit that copy first: its key, then Quit GoodLuck!.

The mailbox will not connect

Use an app password, not your account password, and check that 2-Step Verification is on. The mailbox has to be the address you gave as your contact email on Profile.

Too few jobs

Add more job titles: open Profile, What you want, and click through the suggestions. Check the levels, places and working arrangement you accept, and look under Dropped on Search to see what is being set aside and why. Check too that every source is on (the ⚟ button on Search), and that the hours in Settings, Run cover the time your computer is on.

Scores look low

Open a job's Why this score, and the CV tab. Add the skills you have but your CV does not mention, or say them in your CV.

The phone stays silent

Open the link to your bot, press Start, then Save again in Settings, Notifications, and press Test.

Anything else

The journal says what the app did and what it could not do. If you are stuck, report a problem. Never post a password, a token or your CV there.

14. Your data

Everything GoodLuck! keeps is on your computer:

  • On a Mac, in ~/Library/Application Support/jobbot.
  • On Windows, in %LOCALAPPDATA%\jobbot.

The passwords are kept in Keychain or Credential Manager rather than in that folder.

  • Moving to another computer: Settings, Export / Import, Export everything writes one file into your Downloads. On the new computer, Import a file takes it back, applications and all. The file holds your passwords too: keep it to yourself, and delete it once it is imported.
  • A new version keeps everything: your data is not in the app.
  • Removing GoodLuck! Settings, Start over clears everything, the passwords in the system's vault included, after leaving a backup on your Desktop (delete it too if you do not need it). Then remove the app (on a Mac, drag it to the Bin; on Windows, uninstall it in Settings, Apps) and delete that folder.

Read the privacy note for what goes over the network, and why.
