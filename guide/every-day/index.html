Ten minutes a day

GoodLuck! does the searching, the sorting and the following up. Your part is to decide, to apply, and to answer.

1. In the morning, open Home

  • Best score: the jobs that reach your mark, newest found first, with open all … in Search at the foot.
  • Last 7 days: new jobs (bright: best score) and applications sent, day by day. Its four-corners button opens the last 30 days, and a table of every day with a total.
  • Applications: where they all stand, Sent today, and Replies · last 14 days.

2. Open a job, and read why it scores

  • Why this score says how many of the things the posting asks for are on your profile, then lists each one: how often the posting asks for it, and the line of your CV that shows it, or nothing on your profile says it.
  • The score is that share, from 0 to 100. Your mark is 70 unless you change it in Settings, General, Score mark.
  • A job with too little in it to measure has no score: read it yourself.

3. Apply, then press I applied

  1. Under Open the original posting, open the link, and apply on the employer's own page.
  2. Back in GoodLuck!, press I applied on the job's page. The job goes on Track, and the mail that comes about it is filed to it.
  3. The posting has closed? Press Expired: it leaves Search, Home and the CV tab. Undo brings it back.
From your phonethe same with /jobs 70+ 7d, /job, and its I applied button.

4. Glance at Track and the Queue

  • Track lists every application, in four groups: They are talking to you, Still inside the reply window, Treated as rejected and Settled.
  • A confirmation, an interview invitation, a rejection or an offer moves an application along by itself, at each round. Scan mail reads the mailbox now.
  • An application that stays silent for 20 days is treated as rejected: GoodLuck!'s inference, not a mail. The ⚟ button on Track changes the 20 days (Treat as rejected after).
  • Queue, when it shows a number, holds the mail GoodLuck! could not settle by itself:
    • a move it proposes: Accept it, or Skip it;
    • a mail it could not read: say what it is (acknowledgement, interview invitation, rejection, offer), or skip it;
    • an application it could not tie to a posting: that is the one, or applied elsewhere.

5. Once a week, the CV tab

The CV tab measures your CV against the jobs in Search. Missing from your CV lists what those jobs keep asking for that your CV never says: how many jobs ask for it, and +N, how many more would reach your mark if your CV said it. Open a row to read what employers wrote.

  • A word that is not a skill: press its ✕, and it stops counting in every score. Not counted lists those, and ↺ counts one again.
  • Save report writes the findings into your Downloads folder: a PDF to read, or Markdown (.md) to give an AI assistant together with your CV.

Add to your CV what you can honestly claim, import it again in Profile, and the scores follow.

That is allLeave the session on and let it watch. It does not get tired, and it does not forget to check.
