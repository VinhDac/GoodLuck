Privacy

GoodLuck! has no account and no server of ours. It runs on your computer, keeps what it knows there, and sends nothing to us. This note says what it keeps, what goes over the network, and why.

What it keeps, and where

  • Your search: your profile and CV, the jobs it found, your applications, what it read in your mailbox, the chat, and its journal. They are in one folder on your computer: %LOCALAPPDATA%\jobbot on Windows, ~/Library/Application Support/jobbot on a Mac.
  • Your passwords and keys: the mailbox's app password, your Telegram bot token, the assistant's key, and the password of any account you let it make on an employer's site are kept in your system's own vault, Credential Manager on Windows and Keychain on a Mac.
  • Copies you make: the file Export everything writes holds everything, your passwords included. Keep it to yourself.

What goes over the network, and why

Job boards

To search, GoodLuck! asks companies' public job boards for their postings. These requests carry nothing about you: the boards see your IP address, as with any visit.

Job sites

To search job sites, it opens them in a browser window of its own, with a profile kept in its folder, and does not sign in. A site receives what such a search sends: your job titles, one of your countries and your levels, with your IP address. All this happens only during the hours set in Settings, Run.

Your mailbox

It reads the job mailbox over an encrypted connection, with your address and app password, to find the mail about your applications and your job alerts, and keeps the sender, subject, date and first lines of each message on your computer. It marks nothing as read, moves nothing, and never sends mail.

Employers' forms (Send CV)

When you press Send CV, or Fill it in, it opens each employer's form in a second browser window, the Apply window, and fills it with your profile's answers and your CV. It leaves the Submit to you, unless you turn its safe mode off. It makes an account on an employer's site only after your yes for that site. In that window you may sign in where you choose; on a job site it only reads where a job's Apply leads, and presses nothing there.

Telegram, if you connect a bot

The messages it sends you go through Telegram's servers: job titles, companies, places, scores and links, your applications and where they stand, the questions a form asks, and the day's numbers. Telegram keeps what it delivers, as it does for any message.

The assistant, if you connect it

What you type in the chat goes to Groq, with what the app fetched to answer it: jobs, companies, scores, your applications and where they stand, and a mail's words when you ask about it. Your profile's answers are not sent. A draft (an answer a form asks you to write, a follow-up, notes for an interview, a line of your CV) sends the posting, your CV's words and the employer's mail, without your name, email, phone or links, and only when you press for it.

Links you click

An original posting, the page where you make an app password, the link to your bot: these open in your own browser. The app does not fetch them.

What it never does

  • No analytics, no tracking, no crash reports, no update checks. Nothing about you or your use of the app is sent to us or to anyone else.
  • No mail sent. An application goes only by your Submit, or by Send CV with safe mode off.
  • Nothing open to the network. The app's windows talk to it at 127.0.0.1, an address only your own computer can reach.

This website

These are plain pages. We set no cookies and use no analytics. The tour plays on made-up names and real numbers; nothing you do on it leaves your browser.
