Who you are

Needed when building CVs and sending applications. Leave blank until you get there.

Full namerequired

Contact emailrequired

Phonerequired

Where you're basedrequired

Decides what counts as near you: postings outside this area are dropped. Also printed on your CV and filled into application forms.

Nationality (as on your passport)required

Many application forms ask it. Filled in, the Send stage answers a question asking WHICH nationality; a Yes/No such as "are you a citizen of this country?" and a country of birth stay yours. Hold more than one? Write them all, separated by commas.

Profile linksoptional

GitHub, portfolio, blog. One per row.

How much notice do you have to give?optional

Postings often ask when you can start. Filled in, the Send stage answers both the notice question and the start-date question with it.

Salary to give when a form asks your expectationoptional

What you WRITE when a form asks your expected salary: not the lowest you'd accept (that one, in «What you want», only filters postings). Filled in, the Send stage answers the question with it; a question about your CURRENT salary stays yours. Empty, the question waits for you.

Educationrequired

For an early-career application this IS the main body of the CV, not a footnote. Include module grades and classification: graduate schemes filter on them directly. ONE DEGREE PER LINE. The END DATE of the newest one is your graduation date: that is the answer the Send stage gives when a form asks for it, so a degree with no end date leaves that question for you.

Certifications and awardsoptional

Often the single strongest signal on an early-career CV, and postings frequently name them outright. A ranked result is worth stating.
